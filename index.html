About

I am Chih-Hsuan (Bella) Yang, who publishes as Chih-Hsuan Yang and goes by Bella Yang, a Postdoctoral Researcher at Argonne National Laboratory, working on multi-agent systems and LLM alignment.

I study how AI agents can collaborate reliably: when agents should communicate, whose feedback should be trusted, and how collaborative trajectories can become learning signals.

My work connects system-level evaluation with the details of agent interaction. I examine when collaboration is worth its cost, whether reviewers and solvers actually make use of one another's feedback, and which messages or agents contribute to successful reasoning.

A central part of this research is AgentsSci, an open research program for measuring multi-agent scientific reasoning across protocol choice, critique uptake, trajectory value, and credit assignment.

My earlier work focused on AI for materials and molecular discovery. I developed generative and multi-fidelity learning methods for molecules, organic electronics, and microstructure design, and co-led BioTrove, a large-scale biodiversity dataset and multimodal AI project. Industry research at Google X and Corteva Agriscience connected these interests to multi-agent and multimodal systems and preference-based language-model post-training.

Precise but Uncoupled: Reviewer Precision Does Not Guarantee Critique Uptake in Multi-Agent Math Reasoning
